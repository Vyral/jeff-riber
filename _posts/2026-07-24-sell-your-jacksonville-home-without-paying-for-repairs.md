You just found out you're moving halfway across the country for a new job. That's exciting, and it's a good thing. Then you realize you've been in this house for 13 or 14 years, you need to sell it to get to your next stop, and it needs a fair amount of work if you're going to get what it's actually worth.

That was the call I got on May 15th at about 10:30 in the morning. I called him back roughly 30 minutes later, and it turned out he'd found us through ChatGPT. In that first conversation, it became clear his timeline was tight. His new job started in less than two months, and he needed the money from the sale to make everything work: the new place, the move, getting the family settled. I scheduled a time to come look at the house immediately.

**The work was straightforward but real.** Walking the property, we came to a quick agreement on what needed to happen to maximize the sale price. New flooring, new paint, replacing some fixtures, plumbing, and electrical, and a deep clean. The kind of things that come up once you've been in a house for a decade plus. None of it was exotic.

**The timing made it nearly impossible.** They were moving almost immediately, with about a week and a half before heading out of town to start the job and settle the family. They didn't have the time or the energy to manage all of it, and they didn't have the money either, because that money was already committed to movers, a new place to live, and carrying two households at once.

**So we took the whole thing over.** We arranged all of the contractors, we arranged all of the payments, and we made sure nothing had to be paid until the house closed. The owner never paid a contractor out of pocket. We started on May 15th, and it sold and closed on July 8th. In under 60 days, we went from nothing to a renovated home, actively listed and marketed, under contract, through inspections, sold, and closed. That's a pretty abnormal outcome in the 2026 Jacksonville market.

**The opposite version happens far more often.** A homeowner decides to move, or life circumstances decide for them, whether that's a job, family, or health. They realize the house needs work to maximize what they get, and then they're stuck, because they don't have the money, or the time, or the energy, or the know-how to figure out who to call. Faced with all those decisions, the usual course of action is to do nothing, and doing nothing typically makes it worse. The house isn't getting better; it's getting worse without the repairs. More time on the market usually means less money at the end. And if you decide to just put it up without the work done, at a moment you didn't think through strategically, you end up frustrated and with less in your pocket.

**Here's the solution we built.** I come out and look at the house. Based on your neighborhood and the market surrounding your home, we determine which items let us spend a dollar and get more than a dollar back in value or sales price. Then we build a simple plan to get that work done, and we arrange with contractors to accept payment at closing. We get estimates for everything, we share them with you, and you approve them before any work happens. It sounds simple, but it took years of trial and error to make it actually functional.

**The goal isn't a whole-home renovation.** We're not coming in to replace the roof, replace the windows, repipe the house, or gut the kitchen and bathrooms. That's not the aim, and for most homes I walk into, it isn't what's needed. Usually, it's smaller cosmetic repairs and improvements that are time-consuming and do cost money, but aren't budget-heavy or slow.

**The real benefit is that you get to move on.** You don't have to make 17 different decisions about what should be done, how, when, and who pays for it. You don't have to play general contractor to get your home on the market. We've created an easy button, where you say yes, we want to maximize what we get from this house, and then you get on with your life.
